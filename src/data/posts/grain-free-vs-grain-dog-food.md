### Grain-Free vs Grain: How to Decide

1. **The DCM question** - The FDA identified a potential link between certain grain-free diets (heavy in peas/lentils/potatoes) and dilated cardiomyopathy. No proven cause yet, but discuss with your vet, especially for at-risk breeds (Golden Retrievers, Dobermans, large breeds).
2. **Grains aren't fillers** - Corn, rice, and oats are digestible energy sources and provide nutrients. 'Grain-free' doesn't mean 'better' or 'more meat.'
3. **Allergies are rare** - True grain allergies in dogs are uncommon; most food allergies are to proteins (beef, dairy). Grain-free won't fix a protein allergy.
4. **Evidence matters** - Brands with feeding trials and veterinary nutritionists (Purina, Hill's, Royal Canin, Iams) have more proof of safety than boutique grain-free startups.
5. **Match to your dog** - Healthy dog with no issues? Either works. History of heart disease in the breed? Lean grain-inclusive with research backing.

## FAQ

**Is grain-free bad for dogs?**

Not proven harmful for all dogs, but the FDA is investigating a possible DCM link in some grain-free diets. For many dogs, grain-inclusive is the safer, evidence-backed choice. Talk to your vet.

**Are grains just fillers?**

No. Grains like rice and oats are digestible carbs that provide energy and nutrients. They're only 'fillers' if poorly processed. Quality matters more than presence.

**Should I switch my dog off grain-free?**

Only if advised by your vet, especially for at-risk breeds or if your dog has signs of heart issues. If your dog thrives on grain-free with no problems, abrupt switching isn't necessary - but a vet check is wise.
