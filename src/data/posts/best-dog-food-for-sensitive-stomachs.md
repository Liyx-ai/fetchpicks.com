## How We Tested

Each food was fed to Luna for 3 weeks with a strict protocol: same treats, same feeding times, same portion. We tracked stool score (on a 1-5 scale from diarrhea to perfect logs), gas incidents, vomiting, appetite, and skin reactions. Because sensitive stomachs are often triggered by specific proteins, we also tested each food's protein source diversity - fewer proteins = fewer triggers.

The key insight from testing: most sensitive-stomach foods fail because they still contain multiple protein sources. Dogs with true sensitivities usually need a single-protein formula.

## How to Choose Dog Food for a Sensitive Stomach

1. **Count the proteins** - If your dog has true food sensitivity, fewer protein sources = fewer triggers. Limited-ingredient foods (single protein) are the gold standard.
2. **Look for prebiotics and probiotics** - Ingredients like beet pulp, chicory root, and added probiotics feed healthy gut bacteria and firm up stool.
3. **Consider the carb** - Rice and barley are highly digestible. Sweet potato works for grain-sensitive dogs. Corn and wheat are harder to digest.
4. **Start with a rotation** - When switching, mix 25% new food into old over 7-10 days. Sudden changes are the #1 cause of 'this food didn't work.'
5. **Rule out the non-food causes** - Stress, parasites, and table scraps cause stomach issues too. A food change won't fix those.

## FAQ

**How do I know if my dog has a food sensitivity?**

Typical signs: chronic loose stool, gas, vomiting after meals, itchy skin, ear infections, and licking paws. If symptoms are constant (not just after treats), food is a likely culprit. Your vet can run an elimination diet trial to confirm.

**How long does it take for a sensitive-stomach food to work?**

Most dogs improve within 1-2 weeks on the right food. If there's no improvement after 3 weeks, the food isn't right - try a different protein source or a limited-ingredient formula.

**Is grain-free better for sensitive stomachs?**

Not necessarily. Grain allergies are actually rare in dogs - protein allergies are far more common. Rice and barley are easily digestible. Grain-free only helps if your dog has a confirmed grain allergy.

**Can I mix wet food into a sensitive-stomach diet?**

Yes, but add it slowly and use a gentle wet food (single protein, limited ingredients). Wet food adds hydration, which helps digestion. Just don't switch proteins - keep the wet and dry versions of the same protein.
