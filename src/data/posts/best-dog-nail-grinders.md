## How to Choose a Dog Nail Grinder

1. **Quiet is king** - Noise is what scares most dogs, not the sensation. Look for grinders rated under 50dB if your dog is anxious.
2. **Match power to nail size** - Big dogs with thick nails need the Dremel or Shernbao. Small dogs need precision, not power.
3. **Safety cap matters** - A guard that stops you grinding too deep protects the quick, especially on dark nails where you can't see it.
4. **Consider the light** - An LED is genuinely useful for seeing the quick on dark nails.
5. **Buy spare bands** - Grinding bands wear out in 1-2 months. Models with included spares save you money.

## FAQ

**Are nail grinders better than clippers?**

For most dogs, yes. Grinders are gentler - no snap pressure, no risk of splitting the nail, and you can smooth the edge after. The downside: they take longer and make a whirring noise that some dogs fear.

**How do I avoid hitting the quick?**

Grind in short passes, checking the nail surface after each one. On dark nails, stop when you see a lighter ring appear in the center (the start of the quick). Or use a grinder with a safety cap that physically prevents over-grinding.

**How often should I grind my dog's nails?**

Every 1-2 weeks for most dogs. If you hear clicking on hard floors, it's time. Regular grinding also helps the quick recede, making future trims easier.

**Can I use a grinder on a nervous dog?**

Yes, with patience. Start by just turning it on near the dog for a few days, then touch the paw without grinding, then grind one nail at a time with treats. The quiet models (OYSD, Casfuy) are best for this.
