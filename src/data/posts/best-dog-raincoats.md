## How We Tested

Each coat went through the same routine: a 40-minute walk in steady rain, a 20-minute walk in a downpour, and a shake-and-dry test afterward. We checked: waterproofing (did the undercoat stay dry?), coverage (belly and chest especially - most coats fail here), freedom of movement (can they trot, sniff, and squat?), ease of putting on (over-the-head vs. buckle designs), and drying time.

The belly test is the one that matters. A coat can look great on top and leave the dog's entire underside soaked.

## How to Choose a Dog Raincoat

1. **Check the belly** - Most dog raincoats fail at the underside. Look for a coat with a real belly flap or strap that covers the chest and groin.
2. **Measure length, not just chest** - Measure from the base of the neck to the base of the tail. A coat that's too short leaves the rear end exposed.
3. **Match insulation to climate** - Pure rain shells for warm weather, insulated coats for cold rain.
4. **Consider the leash setup** - Look for a back leash hole so the leash doesn't tangle with the coat.
5. **Test the closure** - Velcro is easy but fails over time. Buckles and snaps last longer. Over-the-head vs. wrap-around matters for fussy dogs.

## FAQ

**Do dogs actually need raincoats?**

It depends on the dog. Short-haired breeds (Boxers, Greyhounds, Labs with thin coats) get cold and miserable in rain. Double-coated breeds (Huskies, GSDs) stay warm but still get soaked, which means a drying session afterward. If your dog shivers or hates rain, a coat helps.

**Is a raincoat better than a towel?**

For the walk itself, yes. A towel only works when you get home. A coat keeps the dog warm and dry during the walk, which matters on long or cold-weather walks.

**How do I measure my dog for a raincoat?**

Measure the back length (neck base to tail base) and the chest girth (widest point behind the front legs). Use the back length as the primary size guide - it's the dimension most brands size by.

**Can my dog run in a raincoat?**

Good coats are cut for movement. Look for generous shoulder room and a chest strap that holds the coat in place. Coats that ride up or restrict the front legs will slow a running dog down.
