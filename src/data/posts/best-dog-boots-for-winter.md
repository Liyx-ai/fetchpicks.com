## How to Choose Dog Winter Boots

1. **Measure the paw, not the breed** - Place your dog's paw on paper, mark the width and length, and compare to the size chart. Every brand fits differently.
2. **Match the boot to the weather** - Salt and mild cold: light boots like Wagwear or Qumy. Deep cold and snow: Muttluks or Polar Trex.
3. **Check the closure system** - Straps stay on better than velcro for active dogs. Velcro is easier for quick trips.
4. **Consider your dog's tolerance** - Some dogs refuse any boot. Start with balloon-style Pawz to build tolerance.
5. **Buy spares for the first walks** - Expect to lose at least one boot in the first week. Most brands sell singles.

## FAQ

**Do dogs really need winter boots?**

Most dogs need paw protection in cold weather, especially with salt and ice melt on sidewalks. Signs your dog needs boots: lifting paws, limping, licking paws, or stopping mid-walk. Boots also protect against ice balls between toes, which can be painful.

**Why do boots keep falling off?**

The most common cause is the wrong size - boots that are too big slip off, and too small fall off too because they can't grip properly. The closure system matters too: straps hold better than velcro for active dogs.

**Are boots safe for puppies?**

Yes, but let them adjust gradually - start with short walks indoors. Puppies grow fast, so you'll need to size up frequently. Most brands have size ranges that cover puppy feet.

**How do I get my dog used to boots?**

Introduce them indoors for 5-10 minutes at a time, with treats. Let the dog walk around the house in them before going outside. Take them off if the dog shows signs of distress - forcing it creates a negative association.
