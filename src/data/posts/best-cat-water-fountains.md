## How to Choose a Cat Water Fountain

1. **Capacity matters** - One cat: 2-3L (refill weekly). Two cats: 3L+ (refill every 3-4 days). More capacity = less daily fuss.
2. **Noise is a dealbreaker** - Place it where you'll hear it (bedrooms, offices). Submerged pumps are quieter. Rain designs are the quietest.
3. **Ceramic over plastic if you can** - Ceramic doesn't harbor bacteria in micro-scratches like plastic does, and there's no plastic taste. It's heavier and pricier but cleaner.
4. **Filter replacement cost adds up** - Check the filter price before buying. A $30 fountain with $15/month filters is not a bargain.
5. **Cleaning is the real chore** - The best fountain is the one you'll actually clean. Dishwasher-safe parts and simple disassembly win in the long run.

## FAQ

**Are cat fountains worth it?**

If your cat doesn't drink enough (most don't), yes. Fountains increase water intake by making water more appealing, which reduces the risk of urinary crystals and kidney disease - the two most common cat health problems. The moving water also filters out dust and chlorine.

**How often should I clean a cat fountain?**

Full clean (disassemble, scrub, rinse) every 1-2 weeks. Replace the filter every 2-4 weeks depending on the brand and how many cats use it. A dirty fountain is worse than no fountain - bacteria grows fast in warm water.

**My cat ignores the fountain. What do I do?**

Some cats take days or weeks to accept a fountain. Keep the old water bowl out alongside it (never remove it suddenly), place the fountain away from their food (cats prefer drinking away from eating), and try a different flow type - some cats want streams, some want still pools.

**Do fountains work for dogs too?**

Yes - most dog-sized fountains are the same products with bigger reservoirs. If you have both, get a fountain with a raised drinking spout tall enough for the dog and a lower stream for the cat.
