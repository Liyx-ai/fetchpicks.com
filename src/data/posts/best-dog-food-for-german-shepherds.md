## How to Choose Dog Food for a German Shepherd

1. **Protein is non-negotiable** - Adults need at least 22%, and active GSDs do better at 26%+. Working lines can go higher.
2. **Check joint supplements** - Look for guaranteed glucosamine and chondroitin. GSDs are one of the top breeds for hip and elbow dysplasia.
3. **Match calcium to age** - Puppies need a large-breed puppy formula with controlled calcium. Feeding an adult formula to a growing GSD can cause bone problems.
4. **Watch the calorie density** - GSDs love food and are prone to obesity. A high-calorie food (like Orijen) needs strict portion control.
5. **Know the allergy pattern** - If your shepherd itches, licks paws, or gets ear infections, try salmon or lamb before you assume it's environmental.

## FAQ

**How much should a German Shepherd eat per day?**

An adult 70lb GSD needs about 3-4 cups of a mid-calorie food per day, split into two meals. Active working dogs can need 25% more. Feed by the bag's chart, then adjust based on body condition - you should feel ribs but not see them.

**Is grain-free food good for German Shepherds?**

Only if your dog has a confirmed grain allergy, which is rare. Grain-free diets have been linked to dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) in large breeds. A standard food with rice or barley is safer for a GSD.

**When should I switch my GSD puppy to adult food?**

Large-breed puppies should stay on puppy food until around 18-24 months, depending on the breeder and vet recommendation. Switching too early can disrupt bone development.

**My GSD has a sensitive stomach. What should I look for?**

Single protein source, limited ingredients, and live probiotics. Introduce the new food over 7-10 days, mixing 25% new food into the old every couple days.
