### How to Choose Senior Small-Breed Food

1. **Kibble size matters** - Small breeds have small mouths and often dental wear. Look for explicitly 'small breed' formulas with petite kibble.
2. **Joint support is non-negotiable** - Glucosamine, chondroitin, and omega-3s help aging joints. Small breeds are prone to patella and arthritis issues.
3. **Protein for muscle maintenance** - Seniors lose lean mass; aim for 25-35% protein from real meat. Check with your vet if kidney issues exist.
4. **Digestibility** - Senior guts are sensitive. Probiotics, moderate fat, and easily digested grains reduce upset.
5. **Palatability** - Senior dogs often eat less. Warming food slightly or choosing a highly palatable formula prevents dangerous weight loss.

## FAQ

**When should I switch my small dog to senior food?**

Most small breeds benefit from senior formulas around age 8-10 (earlier than large breeds, because they age faster). Signs: weight loss, lethargy, dental wear, or picky eating.

**Is grain-free safe for senior small dogs?**

Grain-free is fine for many dogs, but the FDA has investigated a possible link to heart disease (DCM). Discuss with your vet, especially for breeds prone to heart issues. Grain-inclusive senior formulas are a safe default.

**How much should a senior small dog eat?**

Small seniors need 30-40 calories per pound of body weight daily - less than when young, because metabolism slows. Split into 2-3 small meals to aid digestion.
