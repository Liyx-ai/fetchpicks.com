### How to Choose a No-Pull Harness

1. **Front clip is key** - A front-clip harness redirects pulling force sideways, turning the dog back toward you. Back-clip-only harnesses do NOT stop pulling.
2. **Fit matters** - Measure chest girth behind the front legs. A loose harness lets the dog escape; too tight chafes. Four adjustment points are ideal.
3. **Comfort for long wear** - Padded chest/belly panels prevent chafing on long walks. Mesh is cooler in summer.
4. **Durability** - Metal clips outlast plastic. Look for reinforced stitching at stress points.
5. **Training still required** - A harness reduces pulling but doesn't replace rewarding good behavior. Pair with treats for loose-leash walking.

## FAQ

**Do no-pull harnesses hurt dogs?**

No - front-clip harnesses are designed to be gentle. They steer, not choke. Avoid any harness that puts pressure on the throat. Proper fit prevents chafing.

**Front clip vs back clip - which stops pulling?**

Front clip stops pulling (redirects). Back clip is for control on a trained dog. For a puller, you need at least a front clip.

**How long until pulling stops?**

Most dogs improve within 1-3 weeks of consistent use plus rewards. The harness makes it physically easier; your reinforcement makes it permanent.
